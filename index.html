What you just learned

  1. Helper microbes can protect pine roots from disease.
  2. They need time to settle in first. In real experiments that took about a week.
  3. So they must go on before the disease arrives. Added at the same time as the disease, they lose.
For researchers: the evidence

In Scots pine, pre-inoculating harmless binucleate Rhizoctonia (BNR) 168 hours (7 days) before pathogenic uninucleate Rhizoctonia (UNR) suppressed pathogen DNA, whereas simultaneous inoculation let the pathogen win root sites (Grönberg et al., 2009).

The best result in the review: a consortium of Trichoderma longibrachiatum and Burkholderia stabilis reduced Fusarium oxysporum damping-off severity in Pinus massoniana by 74% (Yu et al., 2024). All 20 reviewed studies were lab, greenhouse or nursery-proximate trials — mature trees remain untested.
