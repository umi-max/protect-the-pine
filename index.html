Protect the Pine

Root-rot spores are drifting toward your pine seedlings. You have a few doses of beneficial microbes that can protect them — but only if the microbes settle on the roots in time.

How to play

  1. 1Tap a tree to release beneficial microbes onto its roots. Each tree uses one dose, and doses are limited.
  2. 2The microbes need about 7 days to settle in. Protection grows as they do — watch the green bar.
  3. 3Watch for spores. A warning shows which tree is targeted and how many days until they land.

What the game was really about

  • Timing decides it. Beneficial microbes need a head start of about a week to settle on the roots and put the tree on alert.
  • Prevention beats reaction. Once you can see the spores, it is usually too late to build protection.
  • It is promising, not proven. All 20 studies behind this game were lab, greenhouse or nursery trials. Mature trees in real forests remain untested.

Did you know? The wrong microbe can backfire

In Norway spruce, the bacterium Streptomyces sp. AcH 505 suppressed the tree's immunity while colonising. When challenged by a Heterobasidion abietinum strain 98% tolerant to its antibiotic, root infection actually increased (Lehr et al., 2007).

Protection builds over 7 days of colonisation (the pre-inoculation window in Grönberg et al., 2009) up to a maximum of 74% less damage (the best suppression in the review, Yu et al., 2024). Those two findings come from different host–pathogen systems, and real outcomes vary with host, pathogen and microbes. Damp patches are a game mechanic, not a finding.
